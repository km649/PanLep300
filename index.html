About

PanLep300 is the first annotated benchmark for automated disease detection in a wild non-human primate. It comprises camera-trap footage of wild western chimpanzees (Pan troglodytes verus) from Cantanhez National Park, Guinea-Bissau, annotated with track-level leprosy labels by expert primatologists.

The dataset supports research in wildlife disease surveillance, long-tailed fine-grained recognition, and camera-trap domain adaptation. It is associated with our CV4Ecology @ ECCV 2026 paper.

Data were collected across 48 camera-trap locations over four years (2022–2025) in CNP, Guinea-Bissau. The dataset includes an ecologically-motivated train/val/test split that withholds named individuals and unseen camera installations for evaluation.

Labels: Advanced (visible leprosy signs) and None (no visible symptoms). Overall positive fraction: 5.4% at crop level (17:1 class imbalance).

Data Access

PanLep300 is available to researchers upon request. Due to the sensitive nature of the data (precise locations of an endangered and disease-affected population), access is granted on a case-by-case basis following review by the data custodians.

Before applying, please note: The data may only be used for non-commercial research purposes. Redistribution is not permitted. Publications using PanLep300 must cite the associated paper. Approved applicants will receive a time-limited link to the dataset.
